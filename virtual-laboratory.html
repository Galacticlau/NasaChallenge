What was observed

In the original experiment the flame's length and width decreased as airflow was lowered. It became a small, nearly circular, blue flame and ultimately extinguished while 2.2 cm/s airflow was maintained. The displayed drawing is not a digitization of NASA flame images.

Sources, discrepancies, and interpretation limits

SAFFIRE-I results: NASA's summary table reports 40.6 cm sample width, 94 cm initial length and 0.37 mm thickness for S1. S2 used approximately 10 cm of S1's remaining material, not a second new 94 cm sample. Both had 20 cm/s airflow. The uploaded PSI-98 beta dashboard's extracted CSV has inconsistent length/width labeling and a thickness unit mismatch; this laboratory follows NASA's published physical dimensions.

BASS-II case GMT45-T4: 2.2 cm-wide SIBAL cloth, oxygen 18.7%, airflow reduced from 10 to 2.2 cm/s, extinguished. NASA's Figures 7.5 and 7.6 document intermediate observations. A sequence of still images does not support calculating continuous flame size from an arbitrary formula.

These two experiments used different samples and arrangements. Their numbers must not be mixed into one predictive model. Neither a 420:70 duration ratio nor the difference in spread rates establishes the isolated effect of flow direction, because the available fuel differed between SAFFIRE-I S1 and S2.
