How to Read the Charts

A quick guide to interpreting the curves

Horizontal Axis (X)

Elapsed time in seconds. Use it to identify when a change occurs.

Vertical Axis (Y)

The sensor reading. Units vary by measurement and include cm/s, °C, psi, and volts.

Curves and Points

Each color identifies a channel. A rise or fall shows a change in the reading. Compare heights only when variables use the same units.

Telemetry · SAFFIRE Instruments

Source: SaffireData worksheet · 105 timestamps

Radiometers 1–4

What it measures: Radiometers detect the radiation reaching each sensor. The four channels let you compare how signals vary across positions or directions in the setup.

How to read it: The horizontal axis shows time in seconds. The vertical axis shows each radiometer’s recorded reading. Compare when the curves change and whether the sensors respond in similar ways. Early values near −4,000 are retained. Without documentation of the scale, they cannot be interpreted as physically negative radiation.

Anemometers and the Tach/100 Column

What it measures: Anemometers record air movement. Tach/100 is a signal associated with fan rotation.

How to read it: The horizontal axis shows time, and the vertical axis shows the reading for each channel. Use the chart to examine changes in airflow and the fan signal during the test. The file does not specify RPM as the unit for Tach/100. Interpreting positive and negative values as directions requires the sensor’s sign convention.

Smoke Wire Voltage and Current

What it measures: V is voltage and I is electrical current for the Smoke Wire channel. They are instrument signals. Measuring smoke concentration would require additional information.

How to read it: The horizontal axis shows time, and the vertical axis shows the electrical signals. Use the chart to identify when signals change and compare their behavior. Negative values are retained because polarity and calibration are undocumented in the file.

S1 and S2 Ignition Channels

What it measures: The voltage (V) and current (I) signals of the ignition circuits for samples 1 and 2. An igniter is the device that starts combustion.

How to read it: Compare when the electrical signals change for each igniter. These channels do not directly measure flame temperature. Calculating instantaneous power requires confirming the measurement method and synchronization.

Environmental Signals · Cygnus

Each sensor retains its own timestamps

Obscuration and Scattering

What it measures: The obscuration channel responds to the light that passes through the air to the detector. The scatterometer responds to light scattered by particles. Both are optical smoke detector channels.

How to read it: The horizontal axis uses each sensor’s own timestamps, and the vertical axis shows output voltage. Changes in the curves indicate changes in the detector’s optical response. Estimating smoke concentration requires calibration. The series have different numbers of data points and should not be aligned by row.

Air Temperature and Pressure

What it measures: The Cygnus environmental measurements are temperature in degrees Celsius (°C) and pressure in psi. Flame temperature and fan differential pressure are separate quantities.

How to read it: Time is on the horizontal axis. Each variable has its own vertical scale because °C and psi are different units. Use the chart to see whether environmental readings stayed stable or varied during the record.

Interpretation and Data Limitations

Findings checked against the supplied sources

S1 / S2 Comparison

S1 used concurrent flow and recorded a burn duration of 420 s. S2 used opposed flow and recorded 70 s. This difference describes the two tests. It cannot establish a cause or be generalized to all microgravity combustion tests.

Radiometers

All four channels record values close to −4,000 during the first 71 records, then change starting at 14,962.497 s. The dashboard displays that pattern. The initial readings cannot be interpreted as physically negative radiation without calibration.

Airflow and Fan

The anemometers start with negative readings and later show positive values. The tachometer also shows extreme initial readings. The original values are retained, and the metadata do not establish the corresponding speeds or RPM.

Smoke and Environment

The detector provides obscuration and scattering voltages. A calibration curve would be needed to derive particle concentration or optical density. Stable temperature and pressure readings alone do not show the absence of smoke or establish an environmental cause.

Thermocouple Channels

The PDF verifies the installation of six sensors at the required locations. The supplied Excel workbook does not contain six corresponding time series. The dashboard can document sensor placement, while plotting flame temperatures at those points would require the missing series.

08 · Variable Dictionary

Definitions based on the names and units in the files

Relative Time (s)

“s” stands for seconds. Relative time is elapsed time from the file’s reference point. It lets you order readings and see whether a signal rises, falls, or holds steady. It is distinct from clock time.

Radiometers 1–4

A radiometer detects radiation. The four channels are separate signals. The workbook does not specify the calibration or final physical unit here, so the chart displays recorded values rather than converting them to energy or temperature. The earliest values near −4,000 require technical interpretation and do not establish physically negative radiation.

Anemometers 1–2 (cm/s)

An anemometer measures air movement. cm/s means centimeters per second. The channels show the recorded air-speed signal. Initial readings are negative. Determining what direction the sign represents requires the sign convention and calibration details.

FanTachometer and Tach/100

A tachometer records rotation of a fan or shaft. Tach/100 appears to be a scaled version of the signal, but the file does not specify the output unit. The dashboard retains the original label because RPM is unconfirmed.

SmokeWireV and SmokeWireI

V is voltage and I is electric current. These are the signals for the element labeled Smoke Wire. The name suggests a connection to the smoke detection system, but the file supplies no calibrated conversion to smoke concentration. Negative signs are retained as recorded.

Sample1Igniter and Sample2Igniter

An igniter starts combustion. V and I are the voltage and current signals for channels 1 and 2. They show recorded electrical behavior during ignition. The signals alone do not establish instantaneous power or flame temperature.

Detector Obscuration (V)

The FIZ 2 smoke detector electrical output associated with how much its optical path is obscured. V stands for volts. A change may indicate a change in the signal received by the sensor. Expressing the reading as smoke density or particle count requires calibration.

Scatterometer / Light Scattering (V)

Another detector signal associated with light scattered by particles. It is plotted in volts. A calibration curve would be needed to derive particle count or concentration.

Cygnus Temperature (°C)

°C means degrees Celsius. The temperature channel in the Cygnus worksheet spans approximately 19.67 to 20.18 °C in these records. This is the channel temperature, which is separate from flame temperature.

Cygnus Pressure (psi)

The pressure describes the gas or air at the sensor. psi means pounds per square inch. The supplied readings range from about 14.472 to 14.537 psi. Fan differential pressure is a separate quantity.

Concurrent and Opposed Flow

Concurrent airflow goes in the same general direction as flame spread. Opposed airflow goes in the other direction. The CSV labels S1 Concurrent and S2 Opposed. The table alone cannot establish that airflow direction caused the difference in burn duration.

Thermocouples and Six Locations

A thermocouple measures temperature. The PDF specifies six sensor locations on the sample and documents their installation. The supplied worksheets contain no identifiable temperature series for those six locations. Plotting flame temperatures would require those series.

How to Interpret a Chart:
  • The horizontal axis shows time. The vertical axis shows the recorded value.
  • A rising curve means the recorded value increased. A falling curve means it decreased.
  • Converting an electrical signal to a physical quantity requires calibration.
  • Two variables changing at the same time do not establish causation.
  • A missing, zero, or extreme value may be due to logging, sensor startup, or a technical condition. Document the reason before making corrections.
